SAROS clinical trial readout calendar, 2026–2028

SAROS is a clinical trial readout calendar. It lists every industry-sponsored Phase 1–3 trial registered on ClinicalTrials.gov with a primary-completion date in 2026, 2027 or 2028, more than ten thousand of them, and joins each trial to what public filings say about its sponsor: cash and runway, who holds the stock, insider trades, FDA decision dates, private funding rounds and NIH awards. It is built from public sources only and refreshed every week. Open the calendar.

What it is for

A trial readout is a scheduled alignment: a registered date on which a company, a drug and a market meet. SAROS does not predict outcomes. It keeps the ledger of when those alignments fall, what is publicly known about each party, and how the dates drift from one week to the next. It is written for specialist biotech investors and for anyone who needs to know what reads out when.

What you can do with it

  • See what reads out when. The timeline and calendar hold every trial by registered primary-completion month. Narrow by phase, status, therapeutic area, modality, sponsor tier, drug target or enrollment; click any bar, cell or sponsor to focus on it.
  • Open a trial's dossier. The next action and the rule behind it, readout confidence with its reasons, the sponsor's cash and runway against the date, who holds the stock, insider trades, the drug's mechanism with a curated note, an editable risk-adjusted NPV sketch, and every competing trial in the same indication.
  • Search by target. KRAS, GLP-1, CD19 and hundreds more, tagged by a curated mechanism lexicon.
  • Check the FDA calendar. PDUFA, advisory-committee and resubmission dates exactly as companies disclosed them in SEC filings, each linked to the filing and the sentence it came from. Dates that moved keep their history.
  • Look inside private sponsors. Roughly two thirds of sponsors are not listed. Where they have filed Regulation D placements, SAROS shows amounts raised, dates, investor counts and named officers; NIH awards show non-dilutive funding.
  • Track what changed. Each weekly build is diffed against the last. Slipped, firmed and new trials are filter flags, and the dossier shows the before and after.

How to read a row

  • Readout is the registered primary-completion date. June and December pile-ups are sponsor placeholders and should be read as "sometime that half".
  • Confidence is a transparent rule score, 0–100, of whether the date will hold. It is for triage, not for pricing.
  • Prior is the historical share of trials at this phase that advance, oncology and non-oncology separately, from Wong, Siah and Lo (2019). A base rate, not a view.
  • Impact and Action are how much a readout could matter and the next step an ordered rule list suggests: Pass, Wait, Review flow, Diligence, Size or Monitor. Every score shows the rule that fired.
  • Float is the sponsor's SEC-reported public float, the market-cap proxy for listed names.

What it will not tell you

  • Registered dates lag real readouts. A primary-completion date is when a readout can first exist, not when it will land; open-label early-phase programs drift the most.
  • Private-round valuations, terms, cap tables and private burn are not public. They are shown as unknown, never estimated.
  • Vendor likelihood-of-approval scores, paid consensus, deal terms and prescription data are out of scope by design.

Why "SAROS"

A saros is the cycle of 18 years, 11 days and 8 hours after which the Sun, Earth and Moon return to the same alignment, so an eclipse repeats with nearly the same geometry. Babylonian astronomers found it by keeping a long, honest ledger of dates; by the time of Thales it could be used to say when the next eclipse would come, without any theory of why. That is the stance taken here. The eclipse in the mark is the moment of the readout, and the moons beneath the timeline are the calendar it runs on.
